When removal is the right call — and when it isn’t

We’ll recommend taking a tree down when:

  • It’s dead or dying. Dead eucalypts only get more brittle, and they can drop heavy limbs with no warning.
  • It has a defect that can’t be managed — a major split, decay at the base, or a root plate that’s lifting.
  • It’s damaging something pruning won’t fix, like the liquid amber in Forrest that kept blocking gutters and causing damp inside the house.
  • It’s in the way of plans — a driveway, an extension or a new garden.

And we’ll tell you when it doesn’t need to come down. One or two heavy limbs can often be reduced instead, a healthy tree crowding the roof may just need clearance pruning, and a dead tree well away from buildings can be kept as habitat.

How a tree comes down depends on the site

The method is written into your quote, so you know how the job will be done before we arrive.

Climbing and rigging

A climber works down the tree, cutting it into manageable sections and lowering them on ropes, so nothing lands on the roof, the fence or the garden.

Elevated work platform

When a tree isn’t safe to climb, like a dead eucalypt, the arborist works from a platform basket instead of the tree. We hire the right platform for the job.

Crane‑assisted

Over houses and sheds, a crane can lift large sections straight up and clear, which is often faster and safer than lowering them.

Directional felling

Where there’s enough clear space, a tree can be felled whole in a planned direction — usually the quickest and most affordable option.

Tree removal: common questions

Ask us something else

How much will it cost?

It depends on the tree: size, species and condition, how close it is to buildings or lines, how easy it is to get gear in, and what you want done with the wood, chips and stump. That’s why we look first. The site assessment is free and your quote is a fixed price for the agreed work — the price we quote is the price you pay.

Do I need a council permit to remove or prune a tree?

Often, yes. Most native vegetation in the Otways is protected, many properties sit under extra overlays (landscape, vegetation or landslip), and since September 2025 some large trees on town blocks need a permit too. Whether your tree needs one depends on the tree and exactly where your property is.

Check with council before work is booked — Colac Otway Shire (03 5232 9400) for most of the region, or Surf Coast Shire (03 5261 0600) for Lorne. If we think a permit is likely when we look at the job, we’ll tell you. Our plain-English permit guide explains the main rules.

Can you work on a tree near power lines?

Sometimes — it depends on which line it is and how close the tree is. Around every power line there’s a No Go Zone (3 metres for most local lines), and only Powercor or crews it authorises may cut trees inside it. Keeping trees clear of the service line to your house is usually the owner’s responsibility; trees near the main street lines are handled by Powercor or council.

Tell us about any lines when you enquire. We’ll check on site, work outside the No Go Zone, and tell you if Powercor needs to be involved first — which can mean a wait or an extra cost. Powercor explains the rules.

Is stump grinding included?

Only if it’s in your quote. We list it separately so you can choose — some people want the ground back straight away; others are happy to leave a low stump. See stump grinding.

Will your machinery damage my lawn or driveway?

We plan where machines travel and where sections land before we start. Tell us about soft ground, septic systems, paving or anything underground, and we’ll plan around it. On tight or sensitive sites, more of the work is done by climbing and rigging.

How soon can you do it?

If a tree is dangerous right now, call 0448 762 058 — the line is answered 24/7. For planned work, we’ll give you a date when we quote.

Are you insured?

Yes. We carry $20 million public liability insurance for arboricultural work, and WorkCover for our staff. Every job is planned with a Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) before work starts.
