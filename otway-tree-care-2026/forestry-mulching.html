Know the limits before you start

Big trees come down first. The mulcher is for scrub and small trees. Larger trees are felled and processed separately.

Weeds come back without follow-up. Mulching knocks blackberry and gorse down to the ground, but their roots survive. Plan for spraying or regular slashing afterwards.

Ground and weather matter. Tracks help on slopes and soft ground, but very steep, rocky or waterlogged sites limit what any machine can safely do. We’ll assess this on site.

Native vegetation is protected. Clearing native plants, even dead ones, often needs a permit. Some bushfire and fence line exemptions apply, with conditions — check with council before we book the work. Our fire-prep guide explains the bushfire rules.

Forestry mulching & land clearing: common questions

Ask us something else

Do I need a permit to clear my block?

Often, if the vegetation is native — including dead native vegetation. There are exemptions, such as limited clearing along boundary fences, maintenance pruning, and the 10/30 and 10/50 bushfire rules around older homes in bushfire-prone areas, but each has conditions, and landslip (erosion) overlays add their own. Check with Colac Otway Shire (or Surf Coast Shire for Lorne) before work is booked, and see our permit guide. We’ll flag anything we think needs checking when we look at the site.

Can I walk or drive on it straight away?

Yes. The mulch forms a layer over the ground that you can walk on straight away, and it helps hold the soil and suppress weeds as it breaks down.

Will it get rid of blackberries for good?

Not on its own. Mulching removes the canes, but blackberry and gorse regrow from the roots, so follow-up spraying or slashing is needed to keep them under control.

How much will it cost?

It depends on the area, how dense and tall the growth is, the slope and access: size, species and condition, how close it is to buildings or lines, how easy it is to get gear in, and what you want done with the wood, chips and stump. That’s why we look first. The site assessment is free and your quote is a fixed price for the agreed work — the price we quote is the price you pay.

What happens to bigger trees in the way?

They’re felled first, then chipped, cut into firewood or left as habitat logs, depending on what you want. That’s all written into the quote.
